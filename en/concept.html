Sphere圈域 kwŏnyŏk

How one concept was carried across generations, and where its meaning shifted

The recognition that international politics is not a single world but is composed of multiple domains, each with its own system of meaning. Introduced by Lee Yong-hee in the 1960s, the term recurs throughout this lineage, moving between geographic, civilisational, and epistemic senses depending on who uses it.

Genealogy of usage

  • Lee Yong-hee · 1962 · General International Politics, vol. 1
    The field of international politics is not singular; spheres with differing rationales and systems of meaning overlap within it. To discuss international politics without asking which sphere one belongs to is to speak from another’s position.

  • Noh Jae-bong · 1988 · An Intellectual History of Korean IR
    Shifts the concept of the sphere onto the question of Korean IR’s self-understanding.
    Kukje Chongchi Nonchong 28
  • Ha Young-sun · 2000s · Conceptual history shift
    The sphere moves from a geographic unit to a route of conceptual transmission. It becomes a framework for asking by what path words such as civilisation, state, and nation arrived, and how they were translated.
    The Introduction of the Concept of Civilisation in Modern Korea, and others
  • Chun Chaesung · 2014 · A Systemic Analysis of East Asian Order shift
    The concern behind the sphere is reformulated in the language of organising principles, replacing anarchy as a single premise with hierarchy and compound organising principles.
    Kukje Chongchi Nonchong
  • Circle of the Fish · 2020s
    Carried forward as a project of globalising IR by exploring ontologies in East Asia and beyond. The sphere is now posed as a question of plural ontologies.
    Collective statement and members’ writings
