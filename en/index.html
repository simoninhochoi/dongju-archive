Dongju Lineage Archive

One current of International Relations in Korea, running from Dongju Lee Yong-hee onward

What it is

A bibliographic archive of one current of International Relations scholarship in Korea, formed after Dongju Lee Yong-hee (1917–1997) and traced here through the lineage of texts its members left behind. It gathers fifteen scholars across five generations, their writings, and the relations among them.

Why "lineage of learning"

These scholars do not share a theory. They differ in how they see international politics, in method, and in conclusion. In that sense they are not a school, since the word presupposes a common doctrine.

Yet a clear continuity runs among them. What was passed on was not theory but a set of questions, a body of texts, and a disposition: the refusal to take Western IR as given and the insistence on asking again from where one stands; the practice of drawing on writings left by earlier generations; and the effort to determine one's own position through scholarship. In the sense of learning and its succession, this is called haktong (學統) — a lineage of learning, as distinct from a school.

Continuity has not run downward only. Ties among contemporaries and collaboration across generations have sustained this tradition as much as teaching has. That is why this archive maps relations as a web of sau (師友) — teachers and friends.

This is the editor's reconstruction

Those included here never called themselves by this name. The label "Dongju lineage," the division into five generations, and the number fifteen are all the editor's. This archive does not transcribe something that already existed; it gathers what was scattered and arranges it, and it could have been arranged otherwise.

Two things nonetheless keep the arrangement from being arbitrary. First, these scholars themselves held a clear awareness of a particular tradition formed after Dongju, and said so in print. Second, their writings repeatedly cite the texts of earlier generations. The evidence lies not in the editor's judgment but in the traces of citation they left.

What it is not

This is not an attempt to establish an exclusive lineage. Absence from these pages does not place anyone outside the tradition, and presence does not make anyone its representative. Dongju and the generations after him trained a great many students; what this archive holds is only the portion whose continuity emerges relatively clearly through citation.

Nor does this site provide full texts. It provides bibliography, summaries, and paths to wherever the originals are held. Its task is not to store materials but to show how scattered things connect.

Editorial notes

  • Scope — only the scholars' own writings. Works about them by others are not included.
  • Relations — teaching relations appear only where documented in academic and supervisory records; unverified ties are not drawn. Collaboration and peer ties are determined by co-authorship, joint projects, and shared institutions; citation ties by recurrent reference in the writings.
  • Statement of concern — the single paragraph summarising each scholar's guiding question is written by the editor.
  • Languages — Korean and English editions are maintained in parallel.
  • Data — the full bibliography is downloadable as JSON and CSV.
  • Corrections — errors, omissions, and objections to relational judgments are welcome.
